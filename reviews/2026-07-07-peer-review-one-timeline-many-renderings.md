## Phase 1 — Independent Reviews

### Reviewer 0 — Editor-in-Chief / PC Chair

**Summary of contribution.** The paper presents *Temporal System*, a Wolfram Language paclet that stores typed musical events on an immutable rational-beat timeline and compiles them, via per-backend renderers, into Csound, MusicXML, OSC, and click-track outputs. The framing — "heterogeneous output as a projection problem," one source of truth, everything else derived — is clear and the historical grounding (Tenney, Koenig, the *music n* orchestra/score split) is genuinely well done for this audience.

**Fit & significance.** Strong fit for ICSC. The Csound renderer is the centerpiece (Sect. 4), the orchestra/score separation is respected, and the click-track section is a nice concrete community-relevant payoff. This is exactly the kind of tool a Csound-conference readership wants to hear about.

**Overall readiness.** The writing is above average for the venue — economical, well-structured, honest about limitations ("lossy projection," "beta," "records fidelity differences instead of pretending projections are lossless," lines 199–208). Figures are informative and, notably, self-generated by the tool.

**Reservations (developed by the panel below):**
1. The title/abstract promise ("many renderings," "heterogeneous output") outruns what is actually complete: one backend (Csound) plus a derivative that *reuses the Csound serializer*. The two genuinely different-format backends are the incomplete ones. (DA / R3)
2. Related-work engagement is thin for the novelty being claimed. (R2)
3. No evaluation beyond a single self-authored fixture, and no discussion of the Wolfram dependency as an adoption barrier. (R1, R3)

**Scores (0–10):** Fit 9 · Originality 6 · Significance 6 · Clarity 8 · Overall 7.
**Tentative recommendation:** Minor revision.

### Reviewer 1 — Methodology (architecture & pipeline soundness)

**Angle:** Is the engineering *sound and reproducible*, and is a single self-authored fixture enough to substantiate the claims?

**Strengths.**
- The core design invariants (Sect. 1, lines 88–95) are clean, mutually consistent, and correctly motivated: rational onsets in beat space; *late conversion* to seconds/Hz at render time; tuning as a session parameter; scores-only; permissive store / restrictive per-backend contract. The "retune to 432 Hz or edit the tempoSpec and every second and frequency regenerates without touching one entity" claim (lines 278–280) follows directly from late conversion and is credible.
- **Reproducibility is a real strength and it was verified** (see grounding note above). This kind of verifiable honesty is rarer than it should be — credit it explicitly in the paper.

**Weaknesses (location + fix).**

1. **"Validation" is asserted, not shown (Sect. 3, line 200; Sect. 2, lines 170–173).** MusicXML is "visually validated against a commercial engraving application" and the timeline figure is "the most complex fixture," but the paper never states *what was checked or how*. The bar is not a user study, but state the test surface concretely: e.g., "the Csound suite comprises *N* fixtures; each renders to a `.csd` that Csound compiles without error; audio was auditioned; MusicXML round-trips into Finale/MuseScore with *X* discrepancies." **Fix:** add 2–3 sentences quantifying the test suite and the MusicXML validation criteria.

2. **The "bound copy" is described as thinner than it is (Sect. 4, lines 294–302).** The text says the renderer "extracts the `instr` block … and emits a bound copy whose p-field is wrapped by the control signal." The actual `TS_bind_sine_osc` in `timeline_example.csd` (lines 37–75) is a full re-derivation of the instrument body — envelope generation, `pcount()` guards, `limit()` clamps, pan math — not a light wrapper. A reader will ask: *if the source `.orc` is reproduced-with-modifications, what guarantees the bound copy stays faithful as that `.orc` evolves?* **Fix:** describe the transformation accurately and state the fidelity guarantee (or acknowledge the coupling as a limitation).

3. **Curves are pre-sampled GEN-2 tables, not evaluated at k-rate (Sect. 4, lines 219–222).** The paper says curves become "k-rate control signals … an f-table reader or a generated `transeg`." In the example every curve is a 512-point GEN-2 table read by `tablei`. Legitimate, but the 512-point resolution / stair-stepping / interpolation behavior is undiscussed. **Fix:** one sentence on sampling resolution and why it's adequate (or configurable).

4. **Tempo model limited to piecewise-linear (Sect. 2, lines 144–148).** `tempoSpec` is `{beat, BPM}` points compiled piecewise-linearly. Continuous accelerando/ritardando (curved tempo) — a common need in mixed music — appears unsupported. **Fix:** state this as a scope boundary.

**Scores:** Rigor 6 · Reproducibility 9 · Technical soundness 8 · Completeness of claims 5.

### Reviewer 2 — Domain (related work & novelty delta)

**Angle:** Is the "one source of truth → many projections" contribution actually *new*, and is prior art fairly engaged?

**Strengths.** The historical lineage (Sect. 1, lines 66–86) is accurate and elegantly used to motivate the score/sound split. Citing Koenig's *Project 1/2*, Tenney, Mathews, and the Csound book situates the work credibly in the *music n* tradition.

**Central weakness — the novelty claim is under-defended.** The thesis — "making one authored timeline the only source of truth and demoting every output file to a derived artifact" (lines 84–86) — is a **well-established architecture**, and the manuscript engages almost none of its closest modern instances:

- **music21** (MIT) — single object model exporting to MusicXML, MIDI, Lilypond, text: precisely "one model, many renderings."
- **abjad** — Python → Lilypond with a persistent score model.
- **Bach / cage / dada** for Max — timeline/score abstraction feeding multiple realizations, squarely in this community's world.
- **MEI + Verovio** — canonical encoding rendered to many targets.
- **Common Music / Nyquist / Symbolic Composer** — score model → multiple back ends, in the direct Csound lineage.

Only OpenMusic (line 83) and Taube's *Common Music* book (line 83) are cited, and neither is *contrasted*. **Without this, the reader cannot tell what Temporal System does that music21/abjad/OpenMusic do not.** The genuine delta appears to be *rational-beat immutability + late conversion + session-parameterized tuning + permissive-store/restrictive-contract split* — plausibly novel *as a specific discipline*, but the paper must argue it against named alternatives.

**Fix (required):** add a short related-work paragraph (or fold into Sect. 1) naming 3–4 of the above and stating, in one sentence each, the differentiator. Highest-value revision for scholarly credibility.

**Secondary:** the `\bibitem{wolfram}` entry (lines 389–390) is defined but never cited in the body — either cite it at first mention of the paclet (line 42) or drop it.

**Scores:** Related-work coverage 4 · Novelty (as argued) 5 · Novelty (as it probably is) 6 · Theoretical framing 7.

### Reviewer 3 — Perspective (practical adoption & cross-disciplinary)

**Angle:** Would a working composer in the Csound community actually adopt this, and what's unsaid about the cost?

**Strengths.** The problem is real and well-chosen: divergent hand-maintained timelines (`.csd`, MusicXML, control stream, click) is a genuine pain in mixed-music production (lines 76–82). The click-track section (Sect. 5) targets a concrete, universally-felt chore ("usually rebuilt by hand in a DAW whenever the score changes," lines 310–311) and is the paper's most immediately persuasive moment.

**Weaknesses.**

1. **The Wolfram Language dependency is never acknowledged as a barrier.** The Csound community is overwhelmingly open-source, cross-platform, and cost-sensitive. A tool that requires a commercial Wolfram license to *author* the source of truth is a serious adoption constraint — yet it appears nowhere as a limitation. This is the elephant in the room for *this* venue. **Fix:** address it honestly (licensing; is a free Wolfram Engine path viable; the emitted `.csd` is usable without Wolfram — which it is). Turning it from an unspoken weakness into a stated trade-off will *strengthen* the paper.

2. **No real-composition case study.** Everything hangs on one synthetic test fixture. The persuasive question for a practitioner is: *has a piece been made with this?* Even one paragraph — "used to produce the click tracks and score for [work X]" — would move this from "plausible" to "proven in use." **Fix:** add a real-world usage sentence if any exists; if not, frame the fixture explicitly as a stress test, not a use case.

3. **Artifact portability.** The committed `timeline_example.csd` hard-codes absolute `#include` paths (`/Users/master/Documents/GitHub/temporal-system/instruments/...`, lines 84–85). Not a paper claim, but if the example is meant to be reproducible by readers, portable/relative include handling (or a note) matters.

**Scores:** Practical impact 7 · Adoption realism 5 · Community relevance 8.

### Reviewer DA — Devil's Advocate

**Strongest counter-argument (steel-man against the paper).**
The title, abstract, and thesis all sell *heterogeneity*: "One Timeline, **Many** Renderings," "heterogeneous musical output," Csound *and* MusicXML *and* OSC *and* click. But the evidence base is almost entirely **homogeneous**. Of the four backends, exactly one — Csound — is complete. MusicXML is "beta … lossy … visually validated" (lines 199–200); OSC is a one-paragraph JSON emitter (lines 201–205) with no worked output shown. The headline demonstration that "a new projection costs one contract" (Sect. 5, line 340) is the **click track — which explicitly reuses `serializeCSnd` unchanged** (line 336) and emits `.csd`. So the cheap new projection is not a new *format* at all; it is a second consumer *inside the Csound family*. The architecture's hard promise — that truly different time encodings (divisions-per-quarter, milliseconds, samples; lines 80–81) fall out of the shared core — is demonstrated exactly *where it is easiest* (another Csound score) and left **unproven precisely where it is hard** (notation, real-time OSC). A skeptic concludes: the paper has shown a well-designed Csound score generator with an aspirational multi-format wrapper, and named it after the wrapper.

**Issue list.**

| Sev. | Dimension | Location | Issue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **MAJOR** | Overclaiming | Title, Abstract (37–58), §5 (336–340) | "Many renderings / heterogeneous output" is substantiated only within the Csound family; the click "new projection" reuses the Csound serializer, so it does not evidence cross-format generality. |
| **MAJOR** | Novelty | §1 (82–86) | "One source of truth, everything derived" is not differentiated from music21/abjad/OpenMusic/MEI (see R2). |
| MINOR | Evidence | §3 (204–205) | OSC backend is claimed but no example output is shown, unlike Csound and click. |
| MINOR | Confirmation bias | §5 (338–340) | "A new projection costs one contract" generalizes from the one case (click) that was structurally cheapest. |
| MINOR | Selection | Fig. 2 (170–185) | "Most complex fixture" is author-selected; complexity ≠ coverage. It exercises many entity *types*, but says nothing about how many *fixtures* the suite has or what it covers. |

**Ignored alternative explanations / paths.** The elegance attributed to the architecture may partly be an artifact of only one backend having to actually satisfy the contract. Contracts are cheap to keep clean when there is one demanding consumer; the design's real test arrives when MusicXML and OSC push incompatible demands back onto Layers I–II — which hasn't happened yet.

**Missing stakeholder perspectives.** Performers/engravers (does the beta MusicXML produce usable parts?); live-electronics practitioners (is millisecond OSC actually latency-viable, or just serializable?); non-Wolfram collaborators.

**"So what?" test.** *Passes*, but narrowly: as a Csound score-generation architecture with disciplined late conversion and stable named instruments, it is a real, useful contribution to *this* community. It does **not** yet earn the general "heterogeneous output" claim.

**Is any issue CRITICAL?** **No.** The core artifact works and is honestly reported; the problems are of *scope and framing*, not validity. Accept-as-is is off the table, but the ceiling is not forced below revision.

## Phase 2 — Editorial Synthesis & Decision

### Consensus across reviewers
- **Well-written, well-scoped, historically literate, and honestly reported** (all five). Reproducibility is a verified strength (R1).
- **Two required fixes, independently reached by three reviewers:** (a) the "many renderings / heterogeneous" claim overshoots the completed evidence, which lives inside the Csound family (EIC, R3-implied, **DA MAJOR**); (b) related work / novelty delta is under-defended (EIC, **R2**, DA MAJOR).

### Disagreement / arbitration
- **How damaging is the overclaim?** DA frames it as near-fatal to the title; EIC and R3 see it as a *reframing* problem, not a soundness problem. **Arbitration:** the architecture is genuinely built for multiple backends and one-and-a-half are done; the honest move is to *reframe the claim to match the evidence* (a Csound-first architecture designed for, and partially extended to, heterogeneous output), not to withdraw it. This is a revision, not a rejection.
- **Journal vs. conference bar.** In a journal this would be **Major Revision** (evaluation + related work are structural gaps). For **ICSC**, where a working, well-presented, community-relevant tool with honest limitations is a normal and welcome contribution, the required fixes are largely *additive and textual*. → **Minor Revision.**

> No Devil's Advocate CRITICAL issue was found → the decision is not blocked below Accept, but two MAJOR issues make Accept-as-is inappropriate.

### Editorial Decision: **MINOR REVISION**

*(Recommended as Accept-with-minor-revisions for the ICSC proceedings. Were this a journal, Major Revision.)*

A well-crafted, honest, and genuinely useful systems paper whose framing currently promises more heterogeneity than its completed backends deliver, and whose novelty is real but under-argued. Both are fixable in the existing page budget without new engineering.
